Play in Unciv

The $product map of Spain can be played in Unciv, the free and open-source Civilization V for Windows, macOS, Linux and Android. It has $width × $height tiles of $tile_km km: the peninsula, the Balearic Islands, the Canary Islands (in their inset, as on the map), Ceuta, Melilla and the neighbouring land of Portugal, France and North Africa. Spain starts in Madrid.

Unciv mod ($zip_name) Map only

On a computer (Windows, macOS, Linux)

  1. Install Unciv as its page says: on Windows, the Unciv.msi installer; on Linux, Flathub or itch.io; on macOS, brew install unciv.
  2. Open Unciv and go to Mods. Press Download mod from URL, paste $url/$zip_href and press Download.
  3. Back in the menu, press Start new game. Under Map Type choose Custom and, under Map file, $map_name. The game turns on the $mod_name mod by itself (you will see it ticked under Extension mods): it brings the Iberian pig and the fire season.
  4. Press Select players from starting locations: you play Spain. Add rivals with the + of the player list; each starts in a large city: $starts.
  5. Press Start game!.

Without the mod: download the map only and save it, named $map_name and without an extension, in the maps folder of Unciv's data (if you run it with java -jar Unciv.jar, that is the folder of the .jar). It shows under Map file and in the map editor. It is the same map without the Iberian pig or the fire season, which only exist with the mod.

On Android

  1. Install Unciv from Google Play or F-Droid.
  2. Open Unciv, go to Mods, press Download mod from URL, paste $url/$zip_href and press Download.
  3. Carry on from step 3 above. Android does not need the separate map: the mod has it inside.

From Unciv's mod browser

The mod is also on GitHub ($mod_repo), so it is listed in the game's mods: go to Mods, look for Spainciv under Downloadable mods (or in the Maps category), pick it and press Download. Updates arrive the same way.

Resources

Each resource comes from data. Minerals go to the tile of their deposit or their mine: a Civilization resource is a deposit, not a working mine, so the large and medium deposits of IGME's BDMIN count even if they are no longer worked (the coal of León, Palencia or Asturias, the uranium of Salamanca or Jaén). Products go to the tile with the largest area of their designation of origin, and horses to the comarcas with the most equines per km². Unciv only allows each resource on certain terrains (coal on plains, grassland, hills or forest; uranium on forest, tundra or marsh); if its tile does not qualify, it goes to a neighbouring one that does, and if none does it is left out. Uranium and aluminum, which fit very few terrains, look up to two tiles further, so their tile can be up to about 50 km from the deposit. There is one per tile, and several deposits of the same thing close together give one. The amounts of the strategic resources are those of a large Unciv deposit.

Left without a tile: $not_placed. Nearly all the stone quarries fall on plains, where Unciv puts no stone, and nearly all the uranium on plains of Salamanca and Sierra Morena with no forest, tundra or marsh within 50 km. These designations found no free tile that takes them: $designations.

The Iberian pig is a luxury resource of this mod's own: Unciv has no pigs. It goes on grassland, plains or hills, is improved with a pasture and gives food and gold. It is a luxury and not a bonus resource (like cattle or sheep) because the four designations protect Iberian ham, a cured product, just as the wine designations protect wine and not grapes.

Fire season

The mod adds an event: every $fire_check turns (on turns 5, 10, 15…), each major civilization may get a great fire on its land; city-states never do. The more tiles it owns, the likelier it is, and hills and mountains weigh more. When it happens, you choose:

  • Send fire brigades: you pay gold, $fire_cost_1 before the Medieval era, $fire_cost_2 from the Medieval era up to the Industrial era and $fire_cost_3 from the Industrial era on, multiplied by the game speed (×0.67 on Quick, ×1.5 on Epic, ×3 on Marathon). It only shows when you have more gold than that.
  • Let it burn out: −$fire_penalty% Production in all your cities for $fire_penalty_turns turns.

AI civilizations choose at random: when they can pay, they send the brigades $fire_ai times in $fire_ai_of.

What is data: EFFIS, the European Forest Fire Information System (Copernicus, CC BY 4.0 licence), mapped $fire_n fires of $fire_ha ha or more in Spain from $fire_from to $fire_to, between $fire_min and $fire_max a season. Of all the area it mapped, $fire_summer burnt in fires that started between June and September. Spain's Ministry for the Ecological Transition calls a fire a great forest fire when it passes 500 ha of forest land; EFFIS perimeters also take in farmland, so these $fire_n are the EFFIS fires of $fire_ha ha or more, not the official count of great fires.

Each of those fires is shared among the tiles its perimeter covers. Spanish land tiles get $fire_on_spain of them, $fire_per_season a season. By class of tile, as Unciv counts them, a hill or mountain tile has about $fire_times times as many great fires as any other land:

The weight is the share of Spain's great fires that falls on one tile of that class: Spain's tiles add up to about $fire_unit.

What is the game's:

  • Convention: one turn is one fire season. An Unciv turn lasts from a month to 60 years, depending on the speed and how far the game has gone, so it cannot be literal.
  • Convention: each season, Spain's great fire lands on your territory with the share of Spain you own, the sum of your tiles' weights over $fire_unit. With all of Spain, one every season.
  • Convention: the check runs every $fire_check turns, with the chance of at least one fire in those $fire_check seasons. Unciv only takes whole percentages, so it comes in $fire_bands steps of 1%.
  • Convention: the penalty lasts $fire_penalty_turns turns, the same as the gap between checks, so two penalties never add up.
  • Convention: the price of the brigades and the −$fire_penalty% Production.
  • Assumption: the land of Portugal, France and North Africa has no EFFIS data on this map and counts with Spain's rates for its class.
